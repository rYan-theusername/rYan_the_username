The Bay wakes up covered by a blanket of fog. Often the fog clears to reveal beautiful blue, comfort and warmth, opportunity: things that make me go "today, a day to remember!" and walk outside with my chin up. Sometimes the day stays gray, or it starts to rain (to pour), or the wind is *really* blowing, and it's hard to press open my apartment's front door. Misery is fog burned off by red fire, to reveal evil shapes I don't understand ... I'm not where I think I am ... and I walk outside to go reluctantly about my day as the realization "I'm not safe" settles in.

By "The Bay," I mean the bay with San Francisco, the one where I live in Berkeley.

This is a COOL BOOK and I had a lot of fun reading it. Each of the three acts starts calm and locks you into a roller-coaster with invisible tracks. And I mean it when I say "locks you into." I was turning pages frantically, experiencing vividly Paul's battles with Annie, himself and addiction.

The pilings - still there, even if covered by water - as a metaphor for painkillers is really creative. It aligns well with how *I* experienced pain and morphine after *my* surgery, too. 

Telling such a rich story with only two characters is impressive. Annie is a freakin' maniac.

I thought that the ending was super satisfying (rare!). Endings are always tough, because you don't really want a good story to end. And, regardless of length or depth, the end is always the same: . 

I ought to watch the movie; most of the people to whom I mention the book say it's *also* fantastic. This *includes* those who read the book first! You ought the read the book and let me know whatcha think about it.
